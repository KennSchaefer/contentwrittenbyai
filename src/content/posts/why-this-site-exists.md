Most arguments about AI-written content are made without evidence. One side says
it's all spam that's ruining search. The other says it's indistinguishable from
human writing and twice as fast. Both sides mostly point at examples that suit them.

This site is an attempt to settle part of that argument the boring way: by doing it,
in public, and keeping records.

## What we're testing

The question is narrow. **Can content written by AI be good?** That means accurate,
genuinely useful, and worth a reader's time, not just grammatical.

"Good" has to be measured somehow, so we'll use more than one yardstick:

- **Readers.** Do people find the articles useful? Do they come back?
- **Search.** Do these pages earn rankings and citations on their merits? We'll
  publish the site's own search data so you don't have to take our word for it.
- **Scrutiny.** Can the content survive fact-checking, blind comparisons against
  human writing, and the AI detectors people rely on?

## The rules

An experiment is only worth something if it's run honestly. So:

1. **Every article says who wrote it.** The model is named on the page.
2. **Every article shows how it was made.** A label at the bottom lists the brief,
   how much a human edited it, and whether the facts were checked.
3. **Every edit is public.** The site lives in a public GitHub repository. You can
   compare what the AI wrote with what was published.
4. **No shortcuts.** No mass-produced thin pages, no rewriting other people's
   reporting, no hiding the AI.

## What comes next

Next up are experiments with real results, such as blind tests between human and AI
writing, AI detectors run against well-known human texts, and the same brief given to
several models side by side. We'll also review AI writing tools and track how search
engines treat AI content.

If AI-written content can't hold up under these conditions, this site will show that
too.
